Integrated Care Pathway

Psychological support throughout the cancer journey

Patients may be referred at diagnosis or at any point when emotional, behavioral or social needs may affect wellbeing or treatment.

1. Identify NeedConcern recognized by the patient, family member, nurse, physician or oncology team.
2. Psychological AssessmentReview emotional wellbeing, coping, family support and immediate concerns.
3. Individualized SupportCounseling, education and coping strategies tailored to the patient's situation.
4. Team CoordinationRelevant concerns are coordinated with oncology, nursing, palliative care and other services.
5. Follow-upOngoing support is provided as needs change during treatment and beyond.
Multidisciplinary Cancer Care

Psychology is part of the treatment team

Psycho-oncology works alongside Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology, Surgery, Nursing, Palliative Care, Nutrition and other services so that psychological concerns can be addressed as part of the overall care plan.

Support may be particularly valuable when a patient is newly diagnosed, preparing for major treatment, experiencing persistent distress, coping with body-image or functional changes, or facing recurrence or advanced disease.

Common reasons for referral

  • Anxiety, fear or difficulty adjusting to a cancer diagnosis.
  • Low mood, emotional distress or loss of motivation.
  • Difficulty coping with treatment or hospitalization.
  • Family conflict, caregiver strain or communication concerns.
  • Fear of recurrence or challenges after treatment.
  • Support during palliative care or advanced disease.

Patient-centered psychological care

The Psychology service is designed to be accessible across the continuum of cancer care. Patients can be referred from outpatient consultation, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery, hospitalization, nurse navigation or palliative care.

  • Confidential psychological assessment and counseling.
  • Support for treatment-related fear and emotional distress.
  • Family and caregiver guidance.
  • Coordination with clinicians when psychological concerns may affect treatment participation or quality of life.
