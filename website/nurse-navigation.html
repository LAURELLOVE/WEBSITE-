Universal Closed-Loop Navigation

Every oncology patient stays connected to the care pathway

Nurse navigation links patients with the appropriate clinical teams, helps coordinate next steps and provides a consistent point of contact across the oncology journey.

1. Entry NavigationThe navigator receives the oncology patient, reviews immediate needs and helps organize the first steps in care.
2. Specialist ConsultationThe patient proceeds to the appropriate oncology specialist and diagnostic services.
3. Return to NavigatorBefore leaving, the patient returns to navigation so recommendations and next steps can be closed and coordinated.
4. Treatment CoordinationNavigation supports appointments and movement between Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology, Surgery, Imaging, Pathology and supportive services.
5. Longitudinal Follow-upThe navigator remains connected with the patient as treatment progresses and follow-up needs change.

What the navigation team does

The nurse navigator helps create continuity across a complex multidisciplinary cancer pathway and reduces the risk that an important recommendation or next appointment is missed.

  • Receives and guides new oncology patients.
  • Helps patients understand where they need to go next.
  • Coordinates movement between oncology specialists and diagnostic services.
  • Ensures patients return to navigation after specialist consultation before leaving COC.
  • Supports treatment scheduling and continuity.
  • Maintains contact through treatment and follow-up.

Why Closed-Loop Navigation Matters

The consultation is not the end of the visit.

At COC, the patient is directed back to the nurse navigator after the specialist consultation. The navigator reviews the recommended next steps and helps connect the patient with the services needed to continue care. This closed-loop approach is designed to improve continuity from diagnosis through treatment and follow-up.

Patient Support

Navigation across the multidisciplinary team

The navigator does not replace the treating clinician. Navigation helps connect the patient to the right clinician and service at the right point in the journey.

01

Reduce fragmentation

Patients may need consultations, imaging, pathology, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery and supportive care. Navigation helps connect these steps.

02

Clarify next steps

Patients and caregivers receive guidance on what comes next after consultations and treatment decisions.

03

Support continuity

Longitudinal navigation provides an ongoing connection to COC as treatment progresses and follow-up needs evolve.
