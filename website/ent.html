Patient Pathway

From specialist assessment to coordinated treatment

Head-and-neck cancer treatment often involves several specialties. COC connects each step so diagnosis, staging and treatment planning can be coordinated efficiently.

1. ENT AssessmentSymptoms, clinical examination and review of prior investigations.
2. ImagingCT, MRI or ultrasound according to the clinical question and disease site.
3. Tissue DiagnosisBiopsy, cytology or surgical pathology to establish a definitive diagnosis.
4. StagingDisease extent is assessed to guide treatment selection and sequencing.
5. Multidisciplinary PlanningENT, surgery, radiation oncology, medical oncology and pathology coordinate care.
6. Treatment & Follow-upSurgery, radiotherapy, systemic therapy or combined treatment as indicated.
Head & Neck Oncology

Integrated treatment for complex disease

Patients with head-and-neck cancers may require surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy or combined-modality treatment. COC coordinates these services within one multidisciplinary program so treatment planning can account for tumor control, swallowing, speech, nutrition, symptom relief and quality of life.

Connected services: ENT, Surgical Oncology, Histopathology, Diagnostic Imaging, Radiation Oncology, Medical Oncology, Nutrition, Pharmacy, Psychology and Palliative Care.

Symptoms that may require ENT evaluation

  • Persistent hoarseness or voice change.
  • Difficulty or pain with swallowing.
  • Persistent sore throat or unexplained ear pain.
  • A neck mass or enlarged lymph node that does not resolve.
  • Persistent nasal obstruction or bleeding.
  • Oral, tongue or throat lesion that does not heal.
  • Thyroid nodule or neck swelling requiring specialist review.
Thyroid radioiodine: when indicated for differentiated thyroid cancer, the radioiodine isotope used for therapy is I-131. Nuclear Medicine is presented separately from routine Diagnostic Imaging on the COC website.
