Oncology Nutrition Services

Nutrition is part of comprehensive cancer care

Cancer and cancer treatment can affect appetite, weight, swallowing, digestion, strength and the ability to maintain adequate nutrition. COC provides individualized assessment and nutrition support throughout the treatment journey.

✓

Nutrition Screening

Early identification of patients at risk for weight loss, reduced intake, malnutrition or nutrition-impact symptoms.

A

Comprehensive Assessment

Structured review of weight history, dietary intake, symptoms, function and treatment-related nutrition concerns, including use of PG-SGA where appropriate.

Tx

Treatment Nutrition

Practical nutrition plans for patients receiving chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery or combined-modality treatment.

+

Advanced Support

Escalation from food-based strategies and oral supplements to enteral or parenteral nutrition when clinically indicated and prescribed.

Nutrition Care Pathway

From screening to follow-up

Nutrition needs may change throughout cancer treatment. The care pathway is designed to identify problems early and adapt support as the patient's condition and treatment evolve.

1. ScreeningIdentify weight loss, reduced intake, swallowing problems, gastrointestinal symptoms and other risk factors.
2. AssessmentReview nutrition status, symptoms, functional impact and current treatment plan.
3. Individual PlanDevelop practical, culturally appropriate nutrition goals and strategies for the patient and family.
4. Clinical CoordinationCoordinate with oncology, pharmacy, nursing, surgery and palliative care when additional intervention is needed.
5. ReassessmentMonitor intake, weight, symptoms and treatment tolerance and adjust the plan as required.
Integrated Support

Helping patients stay strong enough for treatment

Nutrition care at COC focuses on maintaining or improving nutritional status, managing nutrition-impact symptoms and supporting patients through demanding cancer treatments.

The Nutrition service works with Medical Oncology, Radiation Oncology, Surgery, Nursing, Pharmacy, Psychology and Palliative Care so nutrition decisions remain connected to the overall treatment plan.

Common reasons for referral

  • Unintentional weight loss or poor appetite.
  • Difficulty chewing or swallowing.
  • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation or taste changes.
  • Nutrition needs before and after major surgery.
  • Reduced oral intake during chemotherapy or radiotherapy.
  • Need for oral supplements, tube feeding or parenteral nutrition assessment.
  • Nutrition support during palliative and advanced cancer care.

Personalized oncology nutrition

Nutrition plans are individualized according to diagnosis, treatment, symptoms, nutritional status, food tolerance and the patient's overall clinical condition.

  • Nutrition screening and structured assessment.
  • Dietary counseling for patients and caregivers.
  • Symptom-focused strategies to improve intake.
  • Oral nutritional supplementation when appropriate.
  • Coordination of enteral or parenteral support when medically indicated.
  • Ongoing reassessment during treatment and follow-up.
